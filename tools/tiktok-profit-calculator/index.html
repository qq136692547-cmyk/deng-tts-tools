Why TikTok Shop profit math is different

01 / explainer

Three things make TikTok Shop's profit math unique compared to Amazon or eBay:

[ 01 ]

Creator commission is the wildcard

On Amazon or eBay, the marketplace is the only platform taking a cut. On TikTok, you can pay creators 1%–30% on top of TikTok's fees. A product that's profitable at 0% creator commission can flip negative at 25%.

[ 02 ]

Ad spend is higher

TikTok Shop sellers typically spend 10%–20% of revenue on TikTok ads to drive traffic. Amazon sellers can usually get away with less because of organic search. Build ad spend into your unit economics.

[ 03 ]

Returns run higher

TikTok's "try before you buy" culture means returns can run 15%–25%, vs Amazon's 5%–10%. Set the resellable rate to reflect how many returns you can actually resell — this dramatically impacts real profit.

[ 04 ]

Hidden costs add up

Inbound shipping to FBT warehouse and monthly storage fees are easy to forget but can eat 5%–10% of margin. This calculator includes them so your numbers reflect reality.

How the profit calculator works

02 / method

TikTok Shop profit is not one number, it is three. Sellers who quote only the first one are the reason so many “profitable” products turn out not to be.

[ 01 ]

Net profit per unit

sale − TikTok fees − creator commission − COGS − shipping − inbound freight − storage − ad spend

What a clean, unreturned order actually pays you.

[ 02 ]

Effective profit per unit

net profit − non-resellable COGS − refund admin − FBT return handling

What you keep once returns are averaged in. A returned unit you cannot resell costs you the full COGS, not the sale price — which is why high-return SKUs so often look healthy and are not.

[ 03 ]

Margin and ROI

Margin is effective profit ÷ sale price. ROI (direct) measures the return on the costs you control — COGS, shipping, inbound freight, ads and returns. ROI (all) adds platform fees and storage, which answers a different question: how hard is every dollar of total cost working?

What a healthy margin looks like

05 / benchmarks

Effective margin is the number to judge, not net margin — net margin flatters any SKU that gets returned.

Effective marginWhat it means in practice
Below 15%Fragile. One bad returns month, one ad-cost spike, or one FBT tier change wipes it out. Fix the unit economics before you scale volume.
20–30%Workable for a lean operation with no paid creators. You have room to absorb returns but not much room to pay affiliates.
30–40%Healthy. This is the band where a 10–15% creator commission is affordable and the product can still fund its own ads.
Above 40%Room to pay creators and run ads at the same time. Usually means strong COGS, a light product, or a genuinely differentiated offer.

These bands are our reading rule, not a survey. The right target for a specific product depends on its return rate and how much of its volume arrives through creators rather than organic content.

Five inputs that quietly break your profit math

06 / pitfalls

The calculator is only as honest as what you type into it. These are the five entries that go wrong most often.

[ 01 ]

COGS without packaging

Boxes, mailers, inserts, labels and poly bags are COGS. Leaving them out understates cost by $0.20–$0.80 a unit, which is often the whole margin.

[ 02 ]

Inbound freight at zero

Getting inventory into TikTok’s FBT warehouse is a real per-unit cost. Setting it to $0 makes any FBT product look better than it is.

[ 03 ]

Creator commission at 0% while the affiliate plan is open

If your plan is live, some share of orders will carry commission. Model the blended rate, not the rate on the orders you happen to see.

[ 04 ]

Returns left out entirely

A 20% return rate costs roughly four times the return drag of a 5% one. Ignoring returns is the single most common reason a profitable-looking product loses money.

[ 05 ]

Ad spend treated as marketing, not COGS

If ads bring the traffic, ad cost belongs in the per-unit math. As a percentage of sale price — typically 10–20% — it is a permanent line item, not a launch expense.

How to lift margin without raising the price

07 / levers

Four moves that change the output of this calculator without changing what the customer pays.

[ 01 ]

Drop an FBT weight tier

Fulfillment runs $4.28 to $12.67 on a single-item order. Reworking packaging to fall into a lower tier is often worth more than a referral-fee negotiation.

[ 02 ]

Raise units per order

Multi-item orders cost less per unit to fulfill than single-item orders. Bundling shifts FBT from a per-item cost to a per-order one.

[ 03 ]

Tier the creator commission

A flat 20% for everyone is expensive. A 10% base with a 20% tier for creators who actually convert keeps the blended rate down.

[ 04 ]

Raise the resellable rate

The share of returns you can put back into sellable stock drops straight into effective profit. Better photography and clearer listing specs are margin work, not marketing work.

Frequently asked questions

08 / questions
How much profit do I make per unit on TikTok Shop?

With the calculator’s default inputs — a $29.99 sale price, $6.50 COGS, $3.00 shipping, $1.00 inbound freight and 10% ad spend — net profit is $10.02 per unit before returns and $9.81 after a 5% return rate. That is a 32.7% margin, or roughly $981 on 100 units a month. Your own number depends mainly on COGS, your FBT weight tier, and whether you pay creator commission.

Is the 6% referral fee the only fee TikTok charges?

No. The 6% referral fee already includes payment processing, so there is no separate transaction or processing charge. On top of it come FBT fulfillment ($4.28–$12.67 per unit by weight on a single-item order), a refund admin fee of 20% of the referral fee capped at $5 per SKU, FBT return handling at $0.70 or $1.00 per returned unit, monthly storage, and roughly 1% on cross-border orders.

Why is my profit lower than the Seller Center dashboard shows?

The Seller Center reports what settled after TikTok’s commission, but it does not present FBT fulfillment, storage, inbound freight or ad spend as per-unit costs. Once those are deducted, real take-home per unit usually lands well below the dashboard figure — especially for heavier products sitting in a high FBT tier.

How much does creator commission change my margin?

Creator commission is set by you, from 0% to 30%, and it stacks on top of every other fee. On a $29.99 product, a 15% commission removes $4.50 from every sale — about 46% of the $9.81 effective profit in the default example. A product that is healthy at 0% commission can turn marginal at 20%.

Should I include returns in my profit calculation?

Yes. In the default example a 5% return rate costs $0.21 per unit, and a 20% return rate costs roughly four times that. A returned unit you cannot resell costs the full COGS rather than the sale price, so setting the resellable rate to match what actually comes back usable is what separates a forecast from a guess.

What is a good profit margin on TikTok Shop?

As a working rule: below 15% is fragile, 20–30% is workable for a lean operation with no paid creators, 30–40% is healthy and absorbs a creator commission, and above 40% leaves room to fund ads and affiliates at the same time. The right target for a specific product depends on its return rate and how much of its volume is creator-driven.
