Quick answer

On the default product — $29.99, $6.50 COGS, 5% returns — TikTok Shop takes $6.32 in platform fees against Amazon FBA's $9.46, and nets $10.96 vs $9.62: TikTok ahead by $1.33 per unit, a 36.5% margin against 32.1%. That lead is fragile, though. Add a 15% creator commission and TikTok falls to $6.46 per unit, letting Amazon win by $3.16. Every figure here assumes the buyer is charged nothing for shipping on either channel, so both referral bases are the item price alone.

When TikTok wins, when Amazon wins

01 / decision guide
[ TTS ]

TikTok wins when

You sell a trending, impulse-driven, low-COGS product with strong visual appeal. Video converts well, so organic reach can stand in for part of your ad spend, and FBT is cheap on light items.

[ FBA ]

Amazon wins when

You sell a high-COGS product where organic search — not paid video — drives most sales, or you pay creators heavily. Prime shipping feeds the Buy Box, and long-tail search outlives any trend.

[ 50/50 ]

Run both

Most multi-channel sellers run Amazon for the long-tail organic demand and TikTok Shop for the trend-driven impulse buys. The same SKU, two audiences, two different profit profiles.

The two fee stacks, side by side

02 / fee comparison

Amazon's headline referral fee (15%) is more than double TikTok's (6%, per TikTok's US commission policy). Both rates are charged on the buyer's total price — item plus shipping, and on Amazon also any gift-wrap charge — which is why this tool has a “Shipping charged to buyer” field for each channel. What closes the gap is everything underneath — Amazon's fulfillment and surcharge stack against TikTok's flat FBT rate card.

What actually flips the answer

04 / sensitivity

Nothing above changes except TikTok's creator commission — same price, same COGS, same ads, same returns:

A single input swings the result from "TikTok earns 14% more" to "TikTok earns 80% less." That is the honest answer to "which platform is better" — it depends on terms you control, not on the headline commission rate.

Beyond creator commission, four other levers move the comparison:

  • Weight. FBT is a flat per-unit fee that climbs steeply with weight; an FBA fulfillment fee climbs too, but from a different curve. Heavy products can flip the winner.
  • Organic reach. Every point of TikTok revenue that arrives without paid spend lowers TikTok's effective ad load — the single biggest structural advantage a video-first marketplace offers.
  • COGS. Low COGS suits both, but it matters more on TikTok, where FBT already eats a large share of a cheap product.
  • Search demand. If buyers type the product name into a search bar, Amazon captures that demand for free forever; TikTok has to re-earn it every week.

Five mistakes when comparing the two platforms

05 / pitfalls
  1. Comparing headline commission rates. 6% against 15% is the beginning of the comparison, not the answer. On the default product the totals are $6.32 and $9.46 — a much narrower gap than the percentages suggest.
  2. Leaving out Amazon's surcharges. The fuel surcharge, inbound placement fee and low-inventory fee together add $0.86 per unit here. Any comparison built on "15% referral" alone is understating Amazon.
  3. Assuming TikTok traffic is free. Organic video can carry a launch, but most sellers buy at least part of their reach. That spend lands straight on the bottom line and is why the standard 10% ad assumption is applied to both sides above.
  4. Ignoring the inbound-shipping asymmetry. Amazon spreads inventory across fulfilment centres; TikTok FBT takes bulk into its own warehouse. Here that is $1.20 against $3.00 per unit — a real gap that has nothing to do with commission.
  5. Deciding on fees alone. COGS, product weight and whether demand is search-driven all move the result more than the fee lines do. Run your own numbers before choosing a channel.

FAQ

06 / questions
Is TikTok Shop cheaper than Amazon FBA?

On the default product, yes: $6.32 of TikTok fees against $9.46 of Amazon fees, and $10.96 versus $9.62 in net profit. But it is not a fixed fact — raise TikTok's creator commission to 15% and Amazon takes the lead by $3.16 per unit.

What does Amazon charge that sellers forget to include?

A 3.5% fuel surcharge on the FBA fulfillment fee, an inbound placement fee ($0.36 per unit at the defaults), a low-inventory fee when cover drops under 28 days ($0.36), and a $0.30 minimum referral fee per item (plus a $0.05 closing fee below $10). A "15% referral fee" comparison leaves all of these out.

When does Amazon FBA actually beat TikTok Shop?

When you pay creators significantly, when the product is heavy or cheap, or when demand is search-driven rather than impulse-driven. Amazon also wins on anything where you would otherwise have to buy all of your TikTok traffic.

Does TikTok's 6% referral fee really beat Amazon's 15%?

On headline commission, easily. On the total stack the gap narrows, because TikTok's flat FBT is often larger than its commission while Amazon bundles fulfillment into a smaller number of lines. Compare the totals, not the percentages.

Should I sell on both marketplaces?

Often yes — the two channels draw different demand: search-driven buyers on Amazon, trend-driven impulse buyers on TikTok. Many sellers run Amazon for baseline long-tail volume and TikTok for spikes, on the same SKU with two cost structures.

How do returns affect the comparison?

Both channels are charged the lost COGS on returns that cannot be resold — here $0.16 per unit at a 5% return rate with 50% resellable. TikTok adds an FBT return handling fee on top, and both keep 20% of the referral fee on a refund, capped at $5 per SKU.
