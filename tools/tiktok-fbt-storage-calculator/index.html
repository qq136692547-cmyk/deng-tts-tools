    Quick answer

    The default inputs put 100 units of a 10×8×6 inch box (480 in³ = 0.28 cu ft each) in the warehouse for 240 days. Days 1–60 are free; the bill starts at day 61 and runs through the 181–270 tier at $0.12 per cubic foot per day. That is 27.78 cu ft billed daily, a total of about $358.33 — roughly $3.58 per unit, or 11.9% of the unit price. Clear the same batch by day 60 and storage costs $0.00.

    The official storage rate card

    01 / rates

    Eight age tiers, effective December 15, 2025. Rates are per cubic foot per day — this is the single most misreported number in TikTok Shop fee guides, which usually present it as a monthly figure.

    Storage daysRate (per cu ft per day)What it means
    30 days or lessFreeA fast sell-through pays nothing
    31–60 daysFreeThe free window runs a full 60 days
    61–90 days$0.03The bill starts here
    91–120 days$0.04First of the finer tiers added 2025-12-15
    121–180 days$0.06Twice the day-61 rate
    181–270 days$0.12Four times the day-61 rate
    271–365 days$0.14Past 270 days the card is unchanged
    Over 365 days$0.27Nine times the day-61 rate

    Two official notes travel with the table. Storage is calculated daily at 23:59 on the total cubic feet of all your units, “including both sellable and defective units” — so damaged stock keeps billing until it leaves. And the minimum chargeable volume is 0.001 cubic feet per SKU per day, which only matters for items smaller than a thumbnail.

    What the tiers are for is deciding what to do about stock that is not moving: a discount is paid once, storage is paid every day and the rate accelerates as the unit ages. When discounting beats waiting works one batch through these same tiers.

    Where the cost really comes from

    02 / method
    [ 01 ]

    Volume, not weight

    billable cu ft = L × W × H ÷ 1728

    Storage ignores how heavy the unit is. A dense 6×4×2 inch box costs a fifth of what a 10×8×6 inch box does, for the same product. If your packaging is mostly air, you rent that air by the day.

    [ 02 ]

    Age compounds the rate

    rate(day) rises with how long the unit has sat

    Nothing changes at day 61 except the rate. The same shelf space bills nine times as much by day 366 as it did on day 61, which is why slow movers hurt twice: capital stalled and a rising rent.

    [ 03 ]

    Every unit in the building counts

    Returns, damaged goods and dead SKUs are billed the same as sellable stock. The rate card is explicit about it, so the cheapest storage decision is often disposal or a clearance price rather than another month of rent.

    Reading the two tables

    03 / how to use it

    The day-by-day table splits your holding period into the tiers it crosses, so you can see exactly which band is driving the bill. If almost all of the cost sits in the 181–270 row, the fix is not better packaging — it is selling, discounting or removing the stock.

    The other-ages table prices the identical batch at 30, 60, 90, 180, 270 and 365 days. The jump between the 90-day and 180-day rows is the number to show a supplier or a buying decision: it is the rent you are choosing to pay by ordering more than you can sell.

    One caveat, stated plainly: this models a batch that arrives together and leaves together. Real inventory ages in slices, so if you restock every two weeks your actual bill is a blend of every tier at once. Treat the result as the cost of a decision, not a forecast of your invoice.

    Frequently asked questions

    04 / questions
    How much does TikTok Shop FBT storage cost?

    FBT storage is billed per cubic foot per day, and the first 60 days are free. From day 61 the rate steps up with inventory age: $0.03 per cubic foot per day for days 61–90, $0.04 for 91–120, $0.06 for 121–180, $0.12 for 181–270, $0.14 for 271–365 and $0.27 beyond 365 days. These rates took effect December 15, 2025.

    Is FBT storage charged monthly or daily?

    Daily. The rate card states fees are calculated daily at 23:59 based on the total cubic feet of all your units, including both sellable and defective units. Guides that quote a flat monthly per-cubic-foot figure are converting the mid-tier daily rates to a month — $0.03 to $0.06 a day is about $0.90 to $1.80 a month. The error is not the arithmetic but the word “flat”: past 180 days the same space bills $0.12 a day and past a year $0.27, which no single monthly figure captures.

    How is storage duration counted?

    The rate card defines it as Storage Days = Outbound Time − Inbound Time, so the clock starts when your inventory is received, not when you created the shipment or when it left your supplier.

    Does defective or unsellable inventory still incur storage fees?

    Yes. The official note says storage is billed on the total cubic feet of all your units, including both sellable and defective units. Damaged stock sitting in the warehouse keeps costing money until it is removed or disposed of, and defective units do not earn.

    Why does storage get so much more expensive after 180 days?

    The card prices age deliberately: inventory older than 180 days bills at $0.12 per cubic foot per day, four times the day-61 rate, and over a year it reaches $0.27. On a 0.28 cubic foot unit that is about $0.076 per unit per day — roughly $227 a month on 100 units of dead stock, before you count the capital it ties up.
