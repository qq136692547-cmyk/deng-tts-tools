TikTok Shop Fee Rate Updates — 2026 Changelog

TikTok changes fulfillment and commission rates quietly, often with little notice. This log records every rate-card change we track, with the new numbers and what they mean for your profit per unit. The calculators now include the June 1 FBT customer return handling fee.

What is the US referral fee, actually? Every claim we’ve checked

Search for the TikTok Shop US referral fee and you will find at least five different answers: 6%, 8%, 7.02%, a 2%–8% tier chart, and a flat $0.30 transaction fee that no longer exists. They cannot all be right. Most of these pages cite no official source and carry no verification date — so we checked each claim against TikTok’s own published rate documents and cross-referenced independent sources. This table is updated whenever a claim changes or our verification does.

Quick answer

As of our latest rate check on September 29, 2026, the standard US TikTok Shop referral fee is 6% for most categories and 5% for select fine-jewelry and pre-owned categories — payment processing included, with no separate transaction fee. The “raised to 8% on August 4, 2026” claim remains unconfirmed: TikTok’s public US category table still lists 6%, and we found no official announcement.

September 29, 2026 — Referral base: we checked it again and kept buyer-paid shipping in

We almost got this one wrong. While unifying the calculators we asked whether the 6% referral fee is charged on the order subtotal or on everything the buyer pays, shipping included. Our first reading of several secondary guides pointed to the subtotal. A second pass against TikTok's own wording reversed it.

The ruling: the referral base includes shipping paid by the buyer. TikTok says so in the note attached to its own commission formula — in Chinese on that page, translated here: “buyer payment includes shipping paid by the buyer, net of shipping discounts provided by the seller or TikTok Shop.” TikTok's ads help center describes the buyer's final price the same way, “including any shipping”. We are not counting second-hand guides for this one: we went back to the policy page itself (TikTok Shop US commission policy). Several independent fee write-ups agree. The few that say otherwise either give no source or contradict their own calculators, and none of them outranks TikTok’s own wording. One honest gap: we could only read the Chinese-language version of that policy page — the English version returns a shell to crawlers. If you are signed in to Seller Center, the same note is worth confirming there.

Why publish the reversal: our other three calculators used the sale price only because they had no input for the shipping the buyer paid — their shipping field measures what you spend shipping the order out, not what the buyer pays you for it. Reading a missing input as a deliberate policy decision was the error, and it is exactly the kind that quietly shifts every number a seller trusts. The fix is now shipped: the Profit, ROAS and TikTok-vs-Amazon calculators all take a “Shipping charged to buyer” input alongside their existing shipping-cost field, and the referral base, the revenue base, the margin denominator and the break-even ROAS all follow the buyer’s total payment rather than the item price alone. No published figure moved — every worked example on this site assumes a $0 shipping charge to the buyer, and the new input defaults to $0. One more page had it backwards, and is now corrected: the FAQ on our $20-product fee article answered “do I pay the referral fee on shipping the customer is charged?” with “no” — the opposite of the ruling above. It now says yes, and cites the same note.

And the comparison tool moved with it. TikTok-vs-Amazon charges Amazon’s referral fee on the buyer’s total price too, because Amazon says so in its own words: “An item’s total price includes its list price, as well as shipping costs and any gift-wrapping charges” (Amazon, Referral fees, read 2026-09-29). A comparison that charged one platform on the item price and the other on the buyer’s total would be two different bases wearing the same label, so both channels now take their own “shipping charged to buyer” figure.

That second pass also firmed up our September 28 correction: the same official note gives the flat 6% rate as taking effect from April 1, 2024.

September 29, 2026 — ROAS Calculator: returns are now charged against product cost

The ROAS Calculator used to write off the full sale price of every returned unit, while the Profit and TikTok-vs-Amazon calculators charged returns against product cost. Same product, two answers roughly nine times apart. We closed the gap: this page now charges returned units against COGS — a return costs you the goods, not revenue you never collected.

What changes for you: on the default $29.99 product ($6.50 COGS, 10% creator commission, 5% returns) the return impact drops from $1.55 to $0.38, profit before ad spend rises from $12.62 to $13.79, and the break-even ROAS falls from 2.38× to 2.17×. Every figure on that page was recomputed to match; nothing else on the site moved.

One honest difference remains: the Profit Calculator lets you set a resale rate for returned stock, while this page has no such input, so it treats every returned unit as unsellable. That is the conservative end — if you resell your returns, your real return cost is lower than the number shown here.

September 28, 2026 — Site correction: TikTok US no longer charges a separate transaction fee

TikTok US no longer charges a separate $0.30 per-order transaction fee — the 6% referral rate already includes payment processing. The flat 6% rate took effect on April 1, 2024 and replaced the older 2% + $0.30 structure.

The April 3, 2023 date that circulates on other sites belongs to something else: it is the start of a 2023 promotion that temporarily cut the referral rate to 0%, not the date the transaction fee ended. This entry supersedes our own earlier wording, which repeated that 2023 promotion date as the fee change. We corrected every calculator and article on this site to remove the outdated $0.30 line: the Fee Calculator now hides the transaction-fee row when it is $0, the Profit, ROAS, and TikTok-vs-Amazon calculators show the updated net payout, and all blog copy reflects the single 6% referral (incl. processing) fee stack.

August 4, 2026 — Reported: referral fee raised to 8% (unconfirmed)

Several seller blogs reported that TikTok Shop raised the standard US referral fee for most non-food categories from 6% to 8% on August 4, 2026, with selected beauty and electronics sub-categories supposedly moving to 9.5–11%. Two months later, this remains a reported change, not a confirmed one. TikTok’s public US category table — the page carries a 14 May 2026 stamp, and independent trackers likewise date its last revision to May 2026 — still lists 6%, and we have found no announcement on any official TikTok seller channel.

What each side has on the record:

  • The 8% claim: Delzonic states the increase took effect August 4, 2026. Its “food and grocery stayed near 5%” detail does not hold either: TikTok’s own category table lists Food & Beverages at 6%. BeBold Digital repeats the 8% figure but labels it a “reported change” and notes the official category table still lists 6%. Neither cites a TikTok announcement.
  • The counter-evidence: FactoryJet checked TikTok’s public category table on September 17, 2026 and still found 6%, writing “we could not confirm it.”
  • Our check (September 29, 2026): TikTok’s own category rate table, plus multiple independent trackers — including the Marknology fee page (self-checked Sep 25, 2026) — all describe 6% for most US categories and 5% for select fine-jewelry and pre-owned categories. The sources we used for each figure are listed under Our sources; see also the claim-by-claim table at the top of this page.

What this means for your numbers: nothing changes in our calculators until TikTok’s own rate card moves — that is the standard we hold this log to. If the 8% figure is ever confirmed, we will date the change here, itemize which categories moved, and update every calculator the same day.

Check it yourself: the authoritative number is always the category rate table in your own Seller Center (US shop → Finance or Products → commission overview). If it shows 8% where we show 6%, that is a confirmed change — and we would like to know: the report link is in the footer.

January 12, 2026 — multi-unit fulfillment fee reduction

Effective January 12, 2026, TikTok reduced multi-unit fulfillment fees by up to 24% for orders with 2, 3, and 4+ items in the 0–4 lb weight tiers (source: TikTok Seller Center FBT Rate Card). The July 13 card keeps the discount across all weight tiers — at 4–8 oz the per-unit saving is now up to 57%.
