FBT for 16–150 lb Products: The November 1, 2026 Rate Card, Checked and Modelled

TikTok Shop expanded Fulfilled by TikTok into large and oversized territory on November 1, 2026. That changes the shape of the decision, not just the size of a fee. Here is what moved, which figures we could not independently verify, and how to price a heavy SKU without trusting a number you have not read yourself.

By TTCalc Research Team · November 9, 2026 · 10 min read

If you have been self-shipping anything over 16 lb because FBT would not take it, that constraint is gone as of November 1, 2026. Before you move a single SKU, price the heavy unit against the exact weight band and fee you read from your own Seller Center rate card. The expansion is real, but the per-band amounts are published in a Seller Center document that requires a login, and we could not independently retrieve it during this check. We are not going to print a table of confident-looking dollar figures we cannot stand behind. Instead, this article gives you the structure of the change, an evidence grade for each part of it, and a worksheet where every amount is an input you supply.

Quick answer

On November 1, 2026, TikTok Shop expanded FBT eligibility to large and oversized items up to 150 lb, structured across five new weight bands above 16 lb, and added or itemized regional placement, customer return handling and exit-inventory removal shipping charges. That is the structural finding recorded in TTCalc's own rate updates log, attributed there to the TikTok Seller Center FBT Rate Card (Knowledge ID 3507056320268087). Evidence grade on the per-band dollar amounts: unverified in this session. We did not reproduce them here, and none of them are pre-filled in any calculator on this site. The decision that actually changed is not "how much is the new tier" but "is FBT now a live option for a SKU that previously had no FBT option at all" — and that is a comparison you can run with your own inputs today.

1. What changed on November 1, 2026

The July 13, 2026 FBT rate card already supported bulky products in a heavy band, but the tiers that most lightweight sellers use stopped at 16 lb. Our calculators, and most of the guides on this site, model the 4 oz to 16 lb range because that is what the majority of small-parcel TikTok Shop catalogues actually ship. The November 1 change matters because it turns a hard eligibility wall into a graduated schedule: a 20 lb item and a 90 lb item are no longer both "not supported," they are two different cost problems.

The five new bands are a schedule, not a price

The useful way to read a five-band schedule is not as five numbers but as five cliffs. Each band boundary is a place where a small change in packaged weight or dimensions moves the unit into a different charge. For a heavy SKU, packaging engineering — removing void fill, shaving an inch off a carton, choosing a denser insert — can matter more than negotiating a cent off the unit cost. That is the same lesson as the dimensional-weight rule on lightweight FBT, amplified: at 16 lb and above, the billable weight is large enough that the multiplier effect of a band boundary is visible in your margin within a handful of units.

Placement, returns and removal are three separate decisions

It is tempting to fold all three into one "extra fees" line. Do not. They occur at different times and are driven by different choices:

  • Placement happens on the way in, and is a function of how you distribute inventory across regional fulfilment centres. Concentrating stock may reduce it; spreading stock may reduce delivery time and increase it.
  • Return handling happens on the way back, and depends on the return disposition settings you chose. Inspection and non-inspection are different charges, so your own configuration is an input, not a constant.
  • Exit inventory removal shipping happens when you give up on stock. It is the cost of admitting a SKU is dead — which is why it belongs in the same model as the discount-or-hold decision, not in a footnote.

Because they are timed differently, a single blended "fulfilment surcharge" percentage will misprice both a fast mover and a slow mover. Keep them as separate inputs.

3. Why 16 lb is a different decision, not just a bigger number

Under 16 lb, FBT is mostly a comparison against self-shipping postage and packing labour. Above it, three additional variables start dominating, and none of them scale linearly with the item price.

Dimensional weight stops being a rounding detail

The chargeable-weight rule on TikTok Shop FBT is threshold-based: below both the weight and volume thresholds, actual weight alone bills; above either, dimensional weight governs. For a heavy but bulky item — a floor lamp, a folded fitness bench, a large storage bin — the carton's volume, not the product's mass, is often what decides the band. The divisor used in the published rule is 166 in inches, not the 139 that circulates in generic parcel guides. Getting that divisor wrong overstates billable weight by roughly a fifth on a mid-sized box, which is more than enough to push a SKU into the next band. The full two-branch rule and worked packing examples are in our dimensional weight guide.

Storage becomes a holding cost you can measure

Heavy units cost more to store and more to remove. Storage on FBT is a schedule that climbs with the age of the inventory, not a flat monthly fee, and the free window runs per unit rather than per calendar month. A slow-moving 40 lb SKU therefore burns a different kind of cash than a slow-moving 8 oz SKU: it occupies expensive space, and when you finally clear it, exit-inventory removal shipping is charged per unit. Model what another 30 days of ageing adds to a heavy unit before you commit the batch size. Our dead stock guide works the discount-or-hold decision tier by tier; the November 1 removal-shipping line belongs in that calculation now.

Returns stop being a small reserve

Lightweight sellers often treat returns as a percentage reserve and move on. For a heavy SKU, a single return can carry return handling, inspection, restocking or disposal, and outbound removal shipping — several separate per-unit charges on an item that already cost a lot to get into the network. The refund administration side is separate again: TikTok's commission policy refunds the platform commission minus an administration fee, which is a different calculation from what FBT charges you to physically process the returned unit. Keep the two models apart or you will double-count. Our return rates and costs guide covers the platform side.

5. A worksheet you can actually run

Five steps, each producing one number you can defend.

  1. Get the real inputs. From your Seller Center FBT Rate Card: the band boundaries, the per-unit amount for your band, the placement allocation, the return-handling amount for your disposition setting, and the removal-shipping amount. From your own packing bench: carton length, width, height and actual weight. From your history: expected monthly units and return rate.
  2. Compute billable weight first. Apply the dimensional-weight rule to the carton, not to the product. If the computed billable weight crosses into the next band, that is a packaging problem before it is a pricing problem.
  3. Cost the FBT route. Enter your own band rate and see the fulfilment line on the heavy unit, then add placement, return handling and removal shipping as separate lines.
  4. Add time. Price the ageing schedule on the batch size you are about to send. If the last unit would still be in the network past the free window, the batch is too big.
  5. Run the whole catalogue and the margin. Price a CSV of heavy SKUs in one pass to find which units cross a band, then carry the winners into a per-unit margin that includes creator commission, ad spend and returns. A fulfilment change that looks small on its own can move the break-even ROAS enough to change a bidding decision.

Every one of those tools takes the rate as an input you supply. None of them gained a new default because of the November 1 change, and none of them should. The moment a rate card moves, you change the input — not the tool, and not the article.

6. What to check before you commit inventory

Four checks, in order, before you send a heavy batch into the network:

  1. Confirm eligibility, not just weight. Large and oversized support has dimensional and handling limits. Read the current rate card for the exact limits and for any category exclusions before you assume your carton qualifies.
  2. Re-measure the packed carton. The band is decided by packaged dimensions and billable weight. A supplier's stated product weight is not the number that bills.
  3. Ask which charges are actually on your account. Placement, inspection-based return handling and removal shipping depend on settings you chose. Two sellers with the same SKU can see different lines.
  4. Test demand before you test volume. A heavy SKU that ages into a higher storage tier is expensive to be wrong about. Cost modelling tells you the price you need; it does not tell you the units will sell. 7. Frequently asked questions

Does FBT now accept items up to 150 lb?

That is what our November 1, 2026 rate updates log records: large and oversized support up to 150 lb, split into five expanded bands above 16 lb. We could not independently retrieve the primary rate card in this session, so confirm the current limits and eligibility rules in your own Seller Center before you plan a shipment. Do not treat this article as the authority on the limits.

Why does this article not list the new per-band prices?

Because we could not verify them against a public primary document during this check, and a confident-looking table of unverified numbers is worse than no table. Our log records the structural change; the amounts come from a login-gated Seller Center rate card. Read them there and enter them yourself. No calculator on this site pre-fills any large-tier amount.

Did TTCalc change any calculator default on November 1, 2026?

No. Every duty and fulfilment rate on this site is entered by the user. That is a deliberate policy: when a rate card moves, you change your input rather than us silently changing a default that thousands of saved calculations depend on.

Is dimensional weight still relevant above 16 lb?

Yes, more so. The chargeable-weight rule is threshold-based and the published divisor is 166 in inches, not the 139 seen in generic parcel guides. A bulky but heavy carton can be billed on volume. Re-measure the packed carton and compute billable weight before you decide which band applies.

Should placement, returns and removal shipping be one blended surcharge?

No. They occur on the way in, on the way back, and when you exit stock, and each depends on a different setting you chose. Blending them into one percentage misprices both fast movers and slow movers. Keep them as separate inputs.

Where do I confirm what I was actually charged?

Your own Seller Center settlement lines are the authority. Our rate updates log records each figure we use, the date we read it, and whether it came from TikTok or an independent tracker; figures we cannot trace are labelled rather than folded into the calculators.
