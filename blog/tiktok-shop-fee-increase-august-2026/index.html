TikTok Shop Fee Increase (August 2026): The Reported 8% Rate, Checked

Since late August, seller blogs have been running a worrying headline: TikTok Shop raised the standard US referral fee from 6% to 8%. Some of those articles are careful, some are not, and TikTok’s own documentation has not settled the question either way. This page lays out both sides of the evidence, runs the margin math at each rate, and shows you how to find the number that actually applies to your orders — which is the only one that matters for your pricing.

1. What was reported for August 4, 2026

Five independent seller resources describe the same move: on August 4, 2026, the standard US referral fee for most non-food categories went from 6% to 8%. The reported category spread:

  • Most US non-food categories: 6% → 8%
  • Select beauty & personal care sub-categories: 6% → 9.5–11%
  • Select electronics sub-categories: 6% → 9.5–11%
  • Food & grocery: reported as staying near 5% (TikTok’s own table lists Food & Beverages at 6% — the reports disagree with the documentation here)
  • Jewelry and pre-owned: 5%, unchanged in every account

The reports trace back to Delzonic, BeBold Digital, Moras, DimMath, and Evolve Media. None of them links to an updated official TikTok policy page, because as of early October 2026 none appears to exist.

2. The evidence, graded

This site holds its rate log to one standard: a number moves here when TikTok’s own rate card moves. By that standard the 8% figure is still a reported change, not a confirmed one. TikTok’s public US category rate table — carrying a 14 May 2026 revision stamp — still listed 6% when we hand-checked it on September 29, 2026. Independent checks line up: FactoryJet re-checked the table on September 17 and still found 6%, and Evolve Media, whose author runs a TikTok Shop brand, reports that the Seller Center fee page still showed 6% when they checked on September 15. Fee changes are delivered by email and Seller Center notice under the Seller Terms, so a rate can change while the public academy page lags — that gap is exactly why the dispute has not settled.

The full claim-by-claim breakdown, with every source and counter-source, lives in the claim-by-claim rate log. If TikTok’s rate card moves, the log is updated the same day and so is every calculator on this site.

3. What two points do to a $25 product

Two points of referral fee sounds small. It is not, because the fee applies to the full sale price while your margin is only a slice of it. Take a $25 product with a $9 landed cost, $4.50 of shipping you absorb, and a 15% creator commission:

  • At 6%: referral fee $1.50 → profit $6.25 per unit (25.0% net margin)
  • At 8%: referral fee $2.00 → profit $5.75 per unit (23.0% net margin)
  • Same product, same price: $0.50 less per unit — an 8% reduction in your profit

The absolute number scales with volume: roughly $1,000 a month in added fees at $50,000 GMV, $4,000 at $200,000, $20,000 at $1 million. And because the fee is deducted from revenue while your costs stay fixed, a thinner margin loses proportionally more — a seller running at 12% net drops to 10%, which is a sixth of their profit gone.

4. The reported increase lands hardest on beauty and electronics

Beauty and personal care is already the most commission-heavy vertical on the platform: creators expect 15–20%, and the reports put the referral fee there at 9.5–11%. Stack an 11% referral fee on a 20% creator commission and roughly a third of your sale price is gone before product cost. Electronics face the opposite problem: price points are higher and buyers compare aggressively, so a five-point fee increase on thin percentage margins can erase profitability outright. Food and grocery sellers are the reported winners — the one group the increase did not touch.

5. The new-seller discount just got worth five points

One detail survives the increase in every account: new sellers who make their first sale within 60 days of completing onboarding still get a promotional referral rate — reported at 2–3% for the first 30 days (TikTok has historically run it at 3%). Against a standard rate of 8%, that window is worth about five points; against 6%, it was worth three. The window starts on your first order, not when you feel ready, so have inventory, content, and creator outreach lined up before you trigger it — if the increase is real, testing on the discount is worth more now than it has ever been.

6. What to do before you commit inventory

  • Read your own settlements. Seller Center → Finance → open any settled order from the last two weeks and divide the referral fee line by the order base. That quotient is your rate; no blog, including this one, outranks it.
  • Rebuild contribution margin at both rates. If the unit still clears your target at 8%, the question is moot. If it only clears at 6%, you are running on an assumption a single email from TikTok could delete.
  • Price the creator commission honestly. It is the most adjustable line you control — but cutting it to absorb a fee increase costs content volume, which costs sales. Model it rather than guess.
  • Keep a buffer. Between reported fee changes, return handling, and aged-inventory surcharges, a unit-economics model with no slack is a forecast that breaks the first week it is wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Did TikTok Shop raise its referral fee to 8%?

It is reported, not confirmed. Five independent seller resources describe the increase effective August 4, 2026, but TikTok’s own public category rate table still listed 6% as of late September 2026, and none of the reports link an updated official policy page. The only rate that matters for your business is on your own Seller Center settlement lines.

Q: Which categories were affected?

Per the reports: most US non-food categories to 8%, select beauty and electronics sub-categories to 9.5–11%, food and grocery and jewelry/pre-owned unchanged near 5–6%. Rates vary by sub-category — confirm your exact rate in Seller Center instead of assuming the headline applies to you.

Q: Do the TTCalc calculators use 6% or 8%?

6% — the only number TikTok’s own published documentation still supports. This site moves its calculators when TikTok’s official rate card moves, not when a blog reports a change. If your settlements show 8%, the rate log explains how to report it.
