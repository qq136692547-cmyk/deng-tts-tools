TikTok Shop Fees on a $20 Product: 2026 Numbers, Line by Line

Every TikTok Shop fee guide starts with the same sentence: it is 6%. For a $100 product that is roughly right. For a $20 product it is nowhere close — the real platform cost is 28.6%, because the referral fee scales with price and fulfillment does not.

Quick answer

On a $19.99 product shipped through FBT in the 4–8 oz tier, TikTok takes $5.72 per order: a $1.20 referral fee (6%, payment processing included) plus $4.52 of FBT fulfillment. That is 28.6% of the buyer's total payment. Adding a 10% creator commission takes it to $7.72, or 38.6%. Run your own price and weight in the free TikTok Shop fee calculator.

What TikTok actually takes from a $19.99 order

Two charges apply to a normal US order in the default category. Nothing else is automatic — the transaction fee that older guides quote ended when the flat 6% referral rate took effect in April 2024 and no longer exists as a separate line.

Charge2026 rateOn $19.99
Referral fee (incl. payment processing)6% of the buyer’s total payment (item price plus any shipping the buyer pays)$1.20
FBT fulfillment, 4–8 oz single unit$4.52 per unit$4.52

Read that last line again. On a $19.99 product, fulfillment alone is 3.8× the commission. That inversion is the single most misunderstood thing about TikTok Shop unit economics, and it is why sellers who model their margin as "price minus 6% minus product cost" are usually off by a wide margin.

If the product weighs under 4 oz, fulfillment drops to $4.28 and the platform cost becomes $5.48 (27.4%). If it lands in the 12–16 oz tier it rises to $5.10 and the stack is $6.30 (31.5%). The low-price trap: percentage thinking breaks under $25

Because the referral fee is proportional and fulfillment is flat, the effective platform cost falls as the price rises. The same 4–8 oz unit, four different price points:

$15.00$0.90$4.52$5.4236.1%
$19.99$1.20$4.52$5.7228.6%
$30.00$1.80$4.52$6.3221.1%

A seller pricing at $15 is not paying "half the fees" of a seller pricing at $30. They are paying 36.1% against 21.1%. The cheap product carries a heavier relative burden on the same physical shipment.

This is the arithmetic reason TikTok Shop's strongest categories cluster in the $25–$60 band. Below that, the fixed fulfillment cost eats the margin before the product ever gets a chance to compete on price.

An 18.9% margin is survivable but not comfortable. It leaves nothing for a meaningful creator programme, and a single returns-heavy month pushes it toward zero. Note also what shipping costs here: $3.00 to the customer plus $0.50 inbound is $3.50 of logistics on top of the $4.52 TikTok charges — $8.02 of pure movement cost on a $19.99 sale.

Returns make it worse. On a 5% return rate with half the returns resellable, the effective profit falls to roughly $3.60. Our return cost guide has the category-level numbers.

What a creator commission does to this product

Creator commission is set by the seller, from 1% to 80% (the official range), and it stacks on top of everything above. On a product this thin, the difference between 10% and 20% is the difference between a business and a hobby.

At 20% the product loses money on every sale — before returns, before any ad-cost variance. The average US commission sits near 13% — a third-party estimate, since TikTok publishes no official average — which on this product is a 3.9% margin. Three ways to make a $20 product work

If the product cannot move up in price or down in cost, you are left with structural moves:

  • Drop an FBT weight tier. Fulfillment runs $4.28 to $9.63 across the common single-item range. Lighter packaging or a smaller variant that falls into the next tier down is worth $0.18–$0.48 a unit immediately — often more than any fee negotiation would deliver. See every FBT weight tier we track in 2026.
  • Sell multi-item bundles. FBT is charged per order on multi-item shipments, not per unit, so the second and third unit in an order cost far less to fulfill than the first. A 2-pack at $34.99 can carry a better margin than two single sales at $19.99 — and it lifts you out of the worst part of the curve.
  • Cap the creator rate at what the product can pay. A flat 15% on a 18.9% margin product is not a marketing decision, it is a margin giveaway. Tier it: a low base rate, with a higher rate reserved for creators who actually convert.

One move that looks attractive and usually is not: treating ad spend as a launch expense rather than a permanent cost. If ads bring the traffic, ad cost belongs in the per-unit math at its steady-state rate, not its launch rate. Our break-even ROAS calculator converts a margin into the ROAS your ads must clear.

Frequently asked questions

How much does TikTok Shop take from a $20 sale?

On a $19.99 order in the 4–8 oz FBT tier, TikTok takes $5.72: a $1.20 referral fee (6%, which already includes payment processing) plus $4.52 of FBT fulfillment. That is 28.6% of the buyer's total payment, not 6%. Add a 10% creator commission and the platform-and-creator cost rises to $7.72, or 38.6%.

Why is the fee percentage higher on a cheap product?

Because the referral fee scales with price but fulfillment does not. FBT is charged per unit by weight, so the same $4.52 applies to a $15 product and a $100 one. On a $15 sale the full stack is 36.1% of revenue; on a $100 sale the same math is 10.5%.

Is a $20 product still profitable on TikTok Shop in 2026?

It can be, but the room is thin. In our worked example a $19.99 product with $5.00 COGS, $3.00 shipping, $0.50 inbound freight and 10% ad spend nets $3.77 per unit — an 18.9% margin. Pay a creator 20% and the same product loses $0.23 per unit.

Do I pay the referral fee on shipping that the customer is charged?

Yes. TikTok charges the referral fee on the buyer’s payment, and the note under its US commission policy formula says, in TikTok’s own Chinese wording (page last revised 2026/2/11), translated here, that buyer payment “includes shipping paid by the buyer, net of shipping discounts provided by the seller or TikTok Shop” — so a shipping line you charge the buyer is part of the 6% base. In the worked example above no shipping is charged to the buyer, which is why the base there is the item price alone. What that does not change is what you pay for out of pocket: your own shipping cost, or the FBT fulfillment rate if TikTok ships the order, is still yours.

What is the cheapest way to sell a $20 product on TikTok Shop?

Get the unit into a lower FBT weight tier, sell multi-item bundles so fulfillment is charged per order rather than per unit, and avoid paying a flat creator commission on a product that cannot absorb it. A $15 to $20 product rarely supports more than a 10% creator rate.
