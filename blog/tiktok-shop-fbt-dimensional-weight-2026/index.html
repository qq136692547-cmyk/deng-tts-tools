TikTok Shop FBT Dimensional Weight: the 2 lb / 332 in³ Rule (2026)

Your FBT tier is not set by what your scale says. Since May 1, 2026 it is set by the official chargeable-weight rule — and for bulky-but-light products, the box picks the tier, not the product.

By L.D. Studio · September 30, 2026 · 8 min read

Most sellers model FBT as "weigh the product, read the tier." That mental model was incomplete before, and since May 1, 2026 it has been written down as a two-branch rule in TikTok's own rate card. Under the rule, some units bill on actual weight alone — and everything else bills on the greater of actual weight and a dimensional figure computed from your box. If your product is bulky relative to its weight, the box is the fee.

Quick answer

FBT's official rule, effective May 1, 2026: a unit at or under 2 lb whose packaged volume is at or under 332 in³ bills on actual weight only. Everything else bills on the greater of actual weight and dimensional weight, where dimensional weight is L×W×H÷166. Example: an 8 oz product in a 10×8×6 in box measures 480 in³ → 2.9 lb dimensional → the 2–3 lb tier at $6.27. The same product in a 6×4×2 in box bills at its actual half pound: $4.52. The box is worth $1.75 per unit.

1. The official rule, verbatim

TikTok's FBT Rate Card & FAQ (Seller Center, page last checked 2026-09-30) states the chargeable-weight rules in one block:

“Effective May 1, 2026, If a unit's actual weight is less than or equal to 2lb and its volume (Length × Width × Height) is less than or equal to 332 in³, shipping charges will be based solely on the actual unit weight. If either threshold is exceeded, the chargeable weight is the greater of actual unit weight or dimensional weight. Dimensional weight is calculated using the formula: Length × Width × Height ÷ 166 (in inches).”

Two footnotes matter as much as the rule. First: “The actual unit weight is determined by FBT's warehouse during inbound processing” — their dock scan, not your shipping scale, is the number that bills. Second: eligibility itself ends at 150 lb actual weight and 108 in on the longest side.

2. The two branches, in plain terms

Branch one — light and compact. At or under 2 lb and at or under 332 in³ of packaged volume, only the scale matters. A 12 oz product in a 6×4×2 in box (48 in³) bills at 12 oz no matter what the divisor says, because both thresholds pass. Note the thresholds are joined by and: exceeding either one drops you into branch two.

Branch two — the greater of the two. Once either threshold is exceeded, TikTok compares your actual weight against the dimensional figure and bills whichever is heavier. This is the branch that surprises sellers, because it is triggered by volume alone: a feather-light pillow in a big box crosses 332 in³ long before it crosses 2 lb, and from there its dimensional weight — not its feather weight — picks the tier.

Run your own unit through both branches with the FBT calculator — it implements the rule exactly as written and shows which branch governed.

4. The divisor is 166 — not 139

Some guides quote a divisor of 139 for TikTok FBT. That is the common domestic-parcel dimensional divisor (used by major US carriers), not TikTok's number. The difference is not cosmetic: at 139, our 10×8×6 box would compute 3.45 lb of dimensional weight instead of 2.89 lb — overstating it by roughly 19% and, in this example, still landing in the same tier but with far less headroom. When you are one box-fold away from a tier boundary, a 19% error in the divisor is a real pricing mistake. TikTok's rate card says 166; this site's FBT calculator uses 166.

6. Packing your way back down a tier

Because the box can govern the tier, packaging is margin work. Three moves, in order of payoff:

Shrink the box. Every cubic inch counts once you are in branch two. Going from 10×8×6 to 9×7×5 takes our 8 oz example from 480 in³ (2.89 lb dim) to 315 in³ — under the 332 in³ threshold entirely, back to actual weight. That is the difference between $6.27 and $4.52 on every single-unit order.

Stay under 332 in³ when you can. The threshold is a cliff, not a slope: a box at 330 in³ bills on actual weight, the same product in a box at 334 in³ enters the dimensional contest. If your product is under 2 lb, engineering the packaging under the volume line is the single highest-leverage change available.

Use multi-unit orders where the buyer allows it. The rate card drops sharply for orders of the same seller's items in one checkout — the lightest tier falls from $4.28 to $1.56 per unit at four items. Bundles do not fix dimensional weight, but they dilute it. The full tier table is in the FBT fulfillment guide.

7. What this means for your margin

A tier is not an abstract classification — it is a per-unit cost line. Moving one tier up typically adds $0.40–$1.00 to every single-unit order; two tiers, double that. On a $20 product running a thin margin, one dimensional-weight surprise can be the whole profit. Two habits keep it honest:

First, weigh and measure the finished parcel, and re-check after any packaging change — remember the warehouse's inbound scan bills, not your estimate. Second, run the number before you set the price: the FBT calculator turns your box into a tier and a fee, and the profit calculator turns the fee into a margin. A product that looks healthy at the 4–8 oz tier can be marginal two tiers up; better to learn that from a calculator than from your first payout statement.

Frequently asked questions

How does FBT calculate chargeable weight?

Two official rules, effective May 1, 2026. Units at or under 2 lb whose volume (length × width × height) is at or under 332 cubic inches are billed on actual weight only. Everything else bills on the greater of actual weight or dimensional weight, where dimensional weight is length × width × height divided by 166.

What divisor does TikTok use for dimensional weight?

166. A 10×8×6 inch box measures 480 cubic inches, and 480 ÷ 166 is 2.9 lb of dimensional weight. Some guides quote 139 — the common domestic-parcel divisor — which would overstate TikTok's dimensional weight by roughly 19%.

Does the box count, or just the product?

The box counts. The official formula uses the dimensions of the packaged unit, and the official footnote adds that the actual unit weight is determined by FBT's warehouse during inbound processing — so the finished parcel, weighed and measured on their dock, is what gets billed.

How large can an FBT item be?

The official eligibility footnote caps units at 150 lb actual weight and 108 inches on the longest side. The standard per-unit rate card runs from 4 oz to 16 lb; items above 50 lb moved to extended heavy/bulky tiers on July 13, 2026, priced on a separate schedule.

Can smaller packaging move me down a tier?

Yes, when dimensional weight governs. The same 8 oz product bills at 2.9 lb dimensional weight ($6.27) in a 10×8×6 inch box, and at its actual half-pound weight ($4.52) in a 6×4×2 inch box. The box alone is worth $1.75 per unit — $175 a month at 100 units.
