8 TikTok Shop Fee Myths, Checked Against TikTok’s Own Pages

Fee guides repeat the same handful of numbers until they feel settled. We ran eight of them back to TikTok’s own documents. Four do not survive, one is half true, and the most expensive error is not a wrong number at all.

By L.D. Studio · September 30, 2026 · 9 min read

Every number below is one we have seen repeated across seller blogs, calculator pages and agency write-ups in 2026. None of them were checked by reading TikTok’s own words, which is how a figure from a different platform’s checkout ends up in a TikTok fee stack. So we did that: this article goes back to the US commission policy (page last revised 2026/2/11), the FBT Rate Card, and TikTok’s Ads help center, and quotes what each one actually says.

Quick answer

Four claims do not match TikTok’s documents: there is no 2.9% + $0.30 transaction fee in the commission policy, the 3% new-seller rate carries no 30-day term, the dimensional divisor is 166 rather than 139, and FBT storage is free for the first 60 days rather than indefinitely. Two more are half true: creator commission runs 1–80% rather than 0–30%, and refunds do return your commission minus a 20% administration fee. The commission is charged on what the buyer pays, shipping included. And the 6% headline is the smallest of the three numbers that decide your margin.

1. “TikTok charges 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction on top of the 6%.”

The commission policy page defines exactly one selling fee. Its own structure is a three-column table — fee type, description, formula — and it has one row: “平台佣金是TikTok Shop针对成功支付和交付的订单收取的服务费” (in TikTok’s Chinese wording, translated here: platform commission is the service fee TikTok Shop charges on successfully paid and delivered orders). The formula under it is commission rate % × (buyer payment + platform discount − taxes), at 6% for most categories and 5–6% by category.

The strings “2.9”, “$0.30”, “transaction fee” (交易费), “handling fee” (手续费) and “payment processing” appear nowhere on the page — we searched the rendered text for each. What 2.9% + $0.30 does match is Shopify Payments’ published US online rate. It is a real number belonging to a different platform, and it travels into TikTok guides whenever the two are compared side by side. We make that same comparison ourselves on the TikTok vs Shopify fee page, where the 2.9% belongs to Shopify’s column, not TikTok’s.

Where we stop short. A page that documents one fee is not the same as a page that declares no other fee exists. What we can defend is narrower and still useful: the fee TikTok documents is a single commission, there is no transaction-fee line in it, and every calculator on this site models exactly that. You will still see the words “includes payment processing” elsewhere on this site — that is our reading rather than TikTok’s, and the rate updates log now labels it as such instead of attributing it to TikTok. If a $0.30 line ever shows up on your settlement statement, your own Seller Center is the authority — and we would want to hear about it, because we would have a correction to publish.

2. “New sellers get 3% for their first 30 days.”

The 3% promotional rate is real — TikTok documents it with its own worked example. The 30 days is the part that is not there. TikTok’s sentence about it reads, in Chinese, “所有参与促销计划的新商家将继续按照各自的计划进行，直到促销期结束” — translated here: all new sellers participating in a promotional plan continue under their respective plan until the promotional period ends. No duration, no start condition, no day count.

The only 30-day period anywhere on that page belongs to a different clause entirely: TikTok undertakes to notify sellers of material changes 30 days before they take effect. Two things that look alike in a summary — a promotional rate and a notice period — got merged into one sentence somewhere, and it has been copied since. If you are pricing your first month on an assumed 3% for 30 days, confirm the actual promotional window inside your own Seller Center before you commit to it.

3. “The referral fee is charged on the item price.”

It is charged on what the buyer pays, and that includes shipping. The official formula takes 买家付款 (buyer payment) as its base, and the footnote attached to the worked example settles what that means — in TikTok’s Chinese, translated here: “买家付款包括买家支付的运费，已扣除商家或TikTok Shop提供的运费折扣” — buyer payment includes shipping paid by the buyer, net of any shipping discount you or TikTok Shop provided.

So a $30 product with $5 of buyer-paid shipping carries commission on $35, not $30: $2.10 instead of $1.80, a $0.30 difference per order that most competing calculators never compute because they only ever ask for a selling price. The fee calculator takes shipping charged to the buyer as its own input, and the full base is set out in what shipping does to the fee base.

4. “Creator commission tops out at 30% (and defaults to 10%).”

TikTok’s Ads help center states the range in English, verbatim: “You can set a commission rate from 1% and 80%.” The 0–30% band that circulates is not TikTok’s number, and there is no published default — you set the rate yourself per product or per collaboration. TikTok also distinguishes the two ways a rate reaches a creator: “Target Collaboration is creator-specific, while Open Collaboration is open to all creators”, and a targeted rate takes precedence over an open one on the same product.

Related, and worth separating from it: TikTok publishes no average commission rate. Any figure presented as “the average” — 13.02% is the one that circulates most — is a third-party sample with a methodology you cannot audit, not an official statistic. To work out what you can actually afford rather than what others pay, run it backwards with the creator commission calculator.

5. “You lose the commission when an order is refunded.”

You get it back, less a fee. TikTok’s wording: the platform commission is refunded to you, minus a 退款管理费 — a refund administration fee — of 20% of that commission. Since 12 May 2025 that fee is capped at $5 per SKU in a single refund, and there are two exemptions: creator sample orders placed through buy-now-refund-later carry no administration fee, and neither do refunds the buyer requests before shipment when the auto-cancel conditions are met.

On the policy page’s own $102 example, a full refund returns $6.30 of commission and keeps $1.26. Model it as 20% of the commission rather than the whole commission and your return reserve shrinks accordingly — the workings are in return rates and what they cost.

6. “FBT dimensional weight divides by 139.”

166. The FBT rate card, effective 1 May 2026, gives the formula in English: “Dimensional weight is calculated using the formula: Length × Width × Height ÷ 166 (in inches).” The 139 that circulates is the common US domestic-parcel divisor, and it does not produce a harmless rounding difference: a 10×8×6 in box is 480 in³, which is 2.89 lb at 166 and 3.45 lb at 139 — about 19% overstated.

There is a second half to this rule that the divisor argument usually loses. Dimensional weight only enters at all once a unit exceeds either 2 lb or 332 in³ of packaged volume; below both, the actual weight alone bills. A guide can quote 166 correctly and still overcharge you by skipping the threshold. Both halves, with worked examples, are in the 2 lb / 332 in³ rule.

7. “FBT storage is free.”

Free for 60 days, then it bills. The rate card: “storage fees for inventory held for the first 60 days will remain free. Effective Dec, 15, we’re further reducing storage fees for inventory stored up to 270 days, offering discounts ranging from 14% to 43% with no timebound end date. Storage fees for inventory stored beyond 270 days will remain unchanged.” Free is a window, not a policy.

The practical shape: 0–60 days free, 61–270 days discounted, past 270 days at the standing rate. Storage is the line that punishes a slow mover quietly — it accrues while you are looking at sell-through, so it belongs in the same model as your per-unit fee. Start with the FBT fulfillment guide, and for stock that has already aged, dead stock: when discounting beats waiting prices the discount-or-hold decision against these tiers.

8. The one that costs the most: “TikTok takes 6%.”

This one is not false, it is incomplete, and incompleteness is more expensive than an error because nothing looks wrong. Take a $19.99 order in the 4–8 oz FBT tier: the commission is $1.20 and FBT is $4.52 — $5.72 in total, which is 28.6% of what the buyer paid. Fulfillment is nearly four times the commission. Any model that treats 6% as “the TikTok fee” has priced under a third of the real platform cost.

Add a creator commission on top — the variable most sellers set last — and the stack moves again. That is the number to protect, and it is why we keep the whole stack in one place rather than a headline rate: the profit calculator models commission, fulfillment, commission to creators and returns together, and the rate updates log shows each figure’s source and the date we read it.

How to check any fee number yourself

Three habits catch nearly all of the above. Ask what the base is — item price or buyer payment with shipping, because the two answers differ by real money on every order. Ask for the date and the document — a rate without an effective date is a rate you cannot tell is current, which is exactly how the retired 2023 figures kept circulating into 2026. And ask whether the number is a commission or a cost — only one of them is 6%.

Where a claim cannot be traced to a TikTok document, we say so on the page that uses it. That is the standard the rate updates log is held to, and it is the reason a few entries there are marked unconfirmed rather than quietly folded into the calculators.

Frequently asked questions

Does TikTok Shop charge a 2.9% + $0.30 transaction fee?

Not in anything TikTok publishes. The US commission policy page defines one selling fee — platform commission at 6% for most categories, 5–6% by category, on (buyer payment + platform discount − taxes). The figures 2.9 and $0.30 do not appear on it; they match Shopify Payments’ published US online rate. One honest limit: a page that documents one fee is not a statement that no other fee exists.

Do new sellers really get 3% for 30 days?

The 3% promotional rate is real and documented; the 30 days is not. TikTok’s own wording is that sellers in a promotional plan continue under that plan until the promotional period ends, with no duration given. The only 30-day period on that page is the notice TikTok gives before material changes take effect.

Is commission charged on the item price or the buyer’s total?

On the buyer’s total, including buyer-paid shipping. The footnote to the official formula states that buyer payment includes shipping paid by the buyer, net of shipping discounts. On a $30 product with $5 of buyer-paid shipping, the base is $35 and the commission is $2.10 rather than $1.80.

What is the maximum creator commission rate?

80%. TikTok’s Ads help center states you can set a commission rate from 1% and 80%. The 0–30% range in many guides is not TikTok’s range, and TikTok publishes no average rate — figures like 13.02% are third-party samples.

Do I get my commission back when an order is refunded?

Yes, minus a refund administration fee of 20% of the commission, capped at $5 per SKU in a single refund since May 12, 2025. Creator sample orders using buy-now-refund-later are exempt, as are pre-shipment refunds that meet the auto-cancel conditions.

What divisor does TikTok use for dimensional weight?

166, and only above a threshold. Dimensional weight is length × width × height ÷ 166 in inches, and it applies once a unit exceeds either 2 lb or 332 cubic inches of packaged volume. Below both, actual weight alone bills.
