1. What USTR Announced on November 6, 2026

In May 2024, the United States Trade Representative (USTR) concluded its four-year statutory review of Section 301 tariffs, granting temporary exclusions for 164 industrial and consumer equipment codes, alongside 14 COVID-related exemptions. These 178 exclusions allowed qualified imports from China to enter at a 0% Section 301 rate (paying only standard Column 1 Most-Favored-Nation duties).

On November 6, 2026, the Office of the United States Trade Representative posted a notice announcing the determination to further extend all 178 current exclusions. Its DATES line reads: “The modifications announced in the annexes to this notice further extend the exclusions through 11:59 p.m. eastern time on January 9, 2027.” The extension applies to goods entered for consumption on or after 12:01 a.m. Eastern on November 10, 2026, and HTS headings 9903.88.69 and 9903.88.70 have their article description changed from “November 9, 2026” to “January 9, 2027”. Evidence grade: verified primary (USTR notice, November 6, 2026; full source list below). Federal Register publication of this notice could not be independently verified as of November 9, 2026, so no FR number is cited. The additional duty on any line not covered by an exclusion is decided by your 10-digit HTS code and entered by you — we do not reproduce a rate table here.

3. Two Scenarios, One Input Sheet: Extension vs. Lapse

The useful question is not “what is the new tariff” but “what changes in my own cost sheet while the exclusion is in force, and what changes if it lapses”. TTCalc pre-fills no duty rate: you enter the additional duty for your 10-digit HTS line, the MFN rate, freight and port charges, and the platform fees from your own Seller Center. Here is the structure, with no invented numbers.

Scenario A — exclusion in force (through 11:59 p.m. Eastern, January 9, 2027):
• FOB goods cost: your input
• MFN base duty: your input, read from your HTS line
• Section 301 additional duty: waived on lines covered by a current exclusion
• Freight, port fees & other import charges: your input
→ Landed cost = the sum of the lines you entered, with no Section 301 layer added on covered lines
Scenario B — if the exclusion lapses (from January 10, 2027):
• Same inputs as Scenario A
• Plus the additional duty for your HTS line, entered by you — do not copy a percentage from any blog, including this one
→ Landed cost = Scenario A plus the Section 301 layer you entered; carry it into your profit model for the new break-even ROAS

The gap between the two scenarios is entirely a number you supply: the additional duty for your own tariff line, applied to your own customs value and volume. 4. Busting the 2026 Tariff Myths

Many sellers and outdated logistics guides are circulating conflicting advice. As verified in TTCalc's Rate Updates Evidence Log, keep these two facts firmly in mind:

  • Myth 1: "Can't we just ship air parcels under Section 321 de minimis ($800)?"
    FALSE. The $800 de minimis exemption for Chinese goods was eliminated on May 2, 2025, and suspended for all countries on August 29, 2025. Every commercial postal and courier shipment into the US is now subject to formal customs entries and duties. The CBP regulation date, statute citation and court ruling quoted in the original version were not independently verified in this session and have been removed.
  • Myth 2: "Don't we also need to add the 2025 IEEPA tariffs on top?"
    FALSE. IEEPA-based tariffs were ruled unlawful in February 2026, and TTCalc deliberately does not model them; several competing calculators still stack dead IEEPA percentages into their results. Section 301 and Section 232 were untouched by that ruling and remain active law. The specific court, refund programme name and case citation quoted in the original version were not independently verified in this session and have been removed.

Which TikTok Shop product categories are most affected?
